# 08 — Design-Type Playbooks: Complete Starting Logic by Category

<aside>
🗂️

These playbooks give KO OS complete starting logic when the user names a design category but does not provide art direction. They are defaults to adapt through the brand—not rigid templates.

</aside>

# 1. Product promotion

**Priority:** product → benefit/offer → CTA → utility.

**Composition:** one hero product at large scale; secondary products smaller and farther back. Use a platform, controlled cluster, hand-held presentation, or contextual use scene.

**Imagery:** preserve packaging, labels, proportions, and functional details. Use three-quarter view for dimensional products; close-up for texture; eye-level product display for clarity.

**Background:** use the brand environment with enough separation around the product. Product colours may inform supporting shapes, but the brand system remains authoritative.

**Footer:** compact order/contact bar or product-stage footer.

**Avoid:** equal emphasis on every product, distorted packaging, hidden labels, and decorative effects that make the item look inaccurate.

# 2. Service promotion

**Priority:** service promise → benefit → proof/context → CTA → contact.

**Composition:** left message/right person is a reliable default. For several services, use a controlled grid with equal card treatment.

**Imagery:** show the service happening, the result, or a credible representative. Generic smiling imagery is weaker than visible service context.

**Background:** clean branded field, subtle workplace or environmental cues, and protected text zone.

**Footer:** full-width contact strip, floating contact bar, or text-only footer for premium services.

**Avoid:** too many service bullets, unrelated stock photography, weak CTA, and tiny contact details.

# 3. Event or webinar

**Priority:** event name → topic/value → speaker → date/time → CTA → sponsors.

**Composition:** stable information column with speaker image opposite; centred stack for a single major event; grid only when several speakers require equal treatment.

**Imagery:** clear speaker portrait, appropriate crop, accurate identity, and expression matching authority or energy.

**Background:** contextual but controlled. Protect dates and registration information from texture.

**Footer:** registration link, sponsor strip, or compact event-information panel.

**Avoid:** treating every detail as a headline, obscuring dates, inconsistent speaker crops, and decorative elements mistaken for information.

# 4. Recruitment or opportunity

**Priority:** role/opportunity → organisation → requirements/benefit → deadline → application CTA.

**Composition:** bold headline above or beside one representative person; use cards for several roles.

**Imagery:** confident, relevant people or team context. Choose gaze and gesture that direct attention toward role information.

**Background:** credible and energetic without appearing like entertainment advertising unless the organisation’s brand requires it.

**Footer:** application URL, email, deadline, and location grouped clearly.

**Avoid:** small deadlines, vague role naming, excessive lifestyle imagery, and decorative icons that resemble requirement bullets.

# 5. School admission or educational programme

**Priority:** admission/programme name → learner level → key benefit → application period → contact.

**Composition:** headline and information on one side; student imagery on the other. Use a grid for subjects, levels, or programme benefits.

**Imagery:** students of the correct age and educational context. Use eye-level or slightly low angles for confidence; warm expressions for approachability.

**Background:** clean learning environment, campus cue, books, technology, or restrained academic motifs.

**Footer:** school contact, location, website, and application action in a stable strip or ribbon.

**Avoid:** incorrect student age, unreadable programme details, excessive childish decoration for older learners, and unverified claims.

# 6. Food or pastry promotion

**Priority:** food/product → appetite appeal → product name/offer → order CTA → contact.

**Composition:** one hero item in the foreground with supporting products behind. Use a three-quarter view for shape and layers, top-down for assortments, or close-up for texture.

**Imagery:** realistic texture, believable glaze, crumbs, fillings, packaging, and serving context. Light should reveal freshness and form.

**Background:** brand-led serving environment or simple product stage. Ingredients may support the story but must not compete with the food.

**Footer:** order bar, delivery information, or compact social/contact panel.

**Avoid:** plastic-looking food, too many equal items, invented discounts, dirty-looking crumbs, and product imagery disconnected from packaging.

# 7. Technology or gadget promotion

**Priority:** headline/benefit → hero device → supporting devices → CTA → contact/trust.

**Composition:** floating product curve, digital platform, or lifestyle user interacting with devices. Keep one device or person dominant.

**Imagery:** crisp screens, accurate ports and forms, believable reflections, and controlled floating shadows. Lifestyle poses should demonstrate use or excitement.

**Background:** digital gradient, radial glow, interface-inspired shapes, or clean modern environment derived from the brand.

**Footer:** interface-style rounded contact panel or standard campaign bar.

**Avoid:** impossible device geometry, too many equally large gadgets, fake logos, mismatched reflections, and effects covering screens.

# 8. Finance, fintech, or app promotion

**Priority:** promise → product interface/card → benefit/proof → CTA → download/access.

**Composition:** phone or card as the product hero; use hands, route lines, receipt confirmations, maps, or lifestyle scenes only when connected to the promise.

**Imagery:** interface must be readable and accurate. Perspective may add energy, but critical UI should not be distorted.

**Background:** trustworthy branded environment with clear hierarchy and controlled depth.

**Footer:** website capsule, app-store badges, sign-up action, or standard download strip.

**Avoid:** generic money imagery with no product connection, inaccurate UI, excessive security claims, and tiny download badges.

# 9. Celebration, holiday, or new-month post

**Priority:** greeting → brand presence → emotional image or motif → optional short wish.

**Composition:** centred greeting, expressive lettering, or lifestyle scene with generous space. The brand should remain visible without turning the post into an aggressive sale unless a promotion is intended.

**Imagery:** select culturally and seasonally relevant motifs. Use restraint; two or three meaningful elements are stronger than a collection of symbols.

**Footer:** often unnecessary. Use a small brand signature or compact contact treatment only when consistent with the campaign.

**Avoid:** overcrowding, unrelated holiday symbols, large sales CTA in a relationship-building post, and weak brand recognition.

# 10. Personal-brand thought leadership

**Priority:** insight/hook → person → supporting statement → name/role → optional CTA.

**Composition:** editorial text-led layout, portrait on one side, or centred quotation system. Preserve repeated portrait treatment and title placement across the series.

**Imagery:** consistent portrait lighting, crop, expression, clothing range, and background treatment. The person is a recognition anchor.

**Background:** restrained enough for reading; recurring shapes or editorial lines may create a series system.

**Footer:** name, role, handle, or small series label—not a heavy commercial footer unless needed.

**Avoid:** changing portrait style every post, decorative quotation marks without function, excessive copy, and typography inconsistent with the person’s positioning.

# 11. Informational or educational graphic

**Priority:** topic → key point → structured supporting information → source or CTA.

**Composition:** title plus numbered sequence, cards, comparison, diagram, or checklist. Use icons only when they aid comprehension.

**Imagery:** secondary to information unless a demonstration is essential.

**Background:** quiet and high-contrast.

**Footer:** source, handle, website, or “save/share” action.

**Avoid:** paragraph-heavy blocks, decorative icons with no meaning, weak grouping, and too many type sizes.

# 12. Offer, discount, or urgent promotion

**Priority:** offer → product/service → deadline/condition → CTA → contact.

**Composition:** offer badge or large numerical statement paired with a clear hero. Keep terms nearby but subordinate.

**Imagery:** the product remains credible; urgency should come from hierarchy and motion rather than visual chaos.

**Footer:** strong action bar with ordering channel and concise terms.

**Avoid:** invented scarcity, hidden conditions, making every element urgent, and overwhelming the product with the discount.

## Cross-playbook rule

For every category:

1. Apply the brand’s fixed anchors.
2. Adapt the playbook to the amount of content.
3. Select one dominant visual idea.
4. Preserve a clear reading order.
5. Use the category’s natural CTA and footer logic.
6. Vary the composition when recent brand outputs use the same family.
7. Evaluate at actual viewing size.

## Core instruction

> The category determines the communication structure; the brand determines how that structure looks and feels; the current message determines what changes.
