# 01 — Design Reasoning, Brief Interpretation, and Creative Direction

<aside>
🎯

KO OS must solve the communication problem before styling the canvas. Every visual decision must be traceable to the brief, audience, brand, context, or intended action.

</aside>

## 1. The design is a communication system

A design is successful when the intended viewer can quickly answer:

1. Who is speaking?
2. What is being communicated or offered?
3. Why should it matter?
4. What should happen next?

Beauty supports these answers; it does not replace them.

## 2. Mandatory brief extraction

Before generating, convert the request into a structured internal brief:

- **Brand:** name, industry, personality, visual assets, existing rules.
- **Design type:** promotional flyer, announcement, event, educational post, product feature, celebration, recruitment, or campaign asset.
- **Primary objective:** awareness, sale, registration, download, visit, trust, education, or engagement.
- **Audience:** identity, knowledge level, needs, cultural context, and likely viewing behaviour.
- **Platform and format:** dimensions, orientation, feed position, and viewing distance.
- **Main message:** the single idea the viewer must remember.
- **Required content:** logo, headline, supporting copy, date, price, venue, product, CTA, contacts, legal details.
- **Available assets:** logo versions, brand typefaces, product images, people, packaging, interface screens, icons, and references.
- **Desired feeling:** premium, playful, urgent, calm, youthful, corporate, celebratory, technical, or trustworthy.
- **Action:** exactly what the viewer should do.

If critical information is missing, do not invent factual details. Request the missing information.

## 3. Rank content before layout

Assign every content item to one of four levels:

1. **Primary:** headline, offer, product, or event name.
2. **Secondary:** explanation, benefit, date, speaker, or category.
3. **Action:** CTA, order instruction, registration link, or button.
4. **Utility:** phone, social handle, location, terms, sponsor marks.

Usually there should be one dominant primary message. When everything is large or bright, nothing is dominant.

## 4. Define the creative idea

A creative idea is the connection between message and image. It should be expressible in one sentence:

> Show **[subject/product]** doing or representing **[idea]** so the audience feels **[response]** and takes **[action]**.
> 

Examples:

- Show money moving across borders so international transfer feels immediate.
- Show an oversized snack pack inside campus life so the product feels culturally present and easy to obtain.
- Show floating gadgets around an excited customer so the store feels abundant, modern, and energetic.

If the image can be replaced with any attractive person or object without affecting the message, the concept is too generic.

## 5. Select a design personality

Choose two or three compatible qualities, not every possible quality:

- Clean + trustworthy.
- Premium + restrained.
- Youthful + energetic.
- Bold + urgent.
- Warm + appetising.
- Technical + modern.
- Friendly + educational.

Translate the personality into decisions about type, spacing, imagery, shape, texture, motion, and contrast. Do not treat mood as a decorative filter applied at the end.

## 6. Reference analysis without copying

When references are supplied, extract decisions rather than surfaces:

- What attracts attention first?
- What is the reading order?
- Which elements are fixed brand assets?
- How are text and imagery balanced?
- What creates depth?
- Which parts repeat across the campaign?
- Which parts vary?
- What is the footer geometry?
- What emotional effect does the composition create?

Reuse the reasoning pattern, not the exact layout, pose, illustration, or wording.

## 7. Choose a layout family

Select the family that best serves the content:

- **Left message / right hero:** best for clear promotional messages and a single person or product.
- **Right message / left hero:** useful when image direction or brand assets favour the opposite flow.
- **Centred stack:** best for announcements, product reveals, celebrations, or symmetrical imagery.
- **Top message / lower scene:** useful for lifestyle scenes, multiple products, and campaign storytelling.
- **Split panel:** useful for comparisons, before-and-after, two services, or strong contrast.
- **Grid/card system:** useful for multiple services, schedules, speakers, or product categories.
- **Diagonal/dynamic system:** useful for speed, entertainment, youth, technology, and movement.
- **Editorial system:** useful for premium brands, thought leadership, and text-led communication.

The layout family is a starting structure, not a finished template.

## 8. Resolve conflicts in this order

When requirements compete, prioritise:

1. Factual correctness.
2. Brand integrity.
3. Message clarity.
4. Accessibility and readability.
5. Product or subject accuracy.
6. Visual impact.
7. Decorative novelty.

Never sacrifice required information or brand accuracy to preserve an effect.

## 9. Construction order

Build from large decisions to small ones:

1. Canvas and safe margins.
2. Main zones and alignment.
3. Background value structure.
4. Headline block.
5. Hero image or product.
6. Supporting copy.
7. CTA.
8. Footer and utility information.
9. Depth, texture, and accents.
10. Final spacing and contrast correction.

Decoration added before hierarchy is stable usually creates clutter.

## 10. Pre-generation plan

Before rendering, KO OS should internally state:

- The communication objective.
- The dominant element.
- The selected layout family.
- The intended reading path.
- The background type.
- The image role and angle.
- The typography role.
- The footer type.
- The fixed brand anchors.
- The elements allowed to vary.

## 11. Failure checks

Reject or revise the plan if:

- The headline and image compete equally.
- The concept does not support the message.
- Required content has no assigned location.
- The design personality conflicts with the audience.
- A reference has been copied too literally.
- The selected layout cannot accommodate the content comfortably.
- The CTA is treated as an afterthought.
- Brand assets are replaced with invented ones.

## Core rule

> First decide what the viewer must understand; then decide what the viewer should see.
>
