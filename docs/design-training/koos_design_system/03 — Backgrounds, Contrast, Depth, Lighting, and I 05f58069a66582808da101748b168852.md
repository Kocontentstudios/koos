# 03 — Backgrounds, Contrast, Depth, Lighting, and Image Integration

<aside>
🌗

The background creates the visual environment, but it must protect the message. Its complexity should be proportional to the amount of information and the strength of the hero image.

</aside>

## 1. Choose the background by function

A background must do at least one of these jobs:

- Create mood.
- Establish context.
- Separate text from imagery.
- Support brand recognition.
- Create depth.
- Direct attention toward the focal point.

If it does not serve one of these jobs, simplify it.

## 2. Light backgrounds

Use a light background when the design needs clarity, openness, softness, trust, food freshness, education, healthcare, corporate restraint, or substantial text.

Construction:

1. Start with a clean light value.
2. Add a faint gradient or low-contrast texture so it does not feel unfinished.
3. Reserve a quiet area for text.
4. Use darker text and defined product or subject edges.
5. Add a soft shadow, outline, or contrasting shape if a light subject disappears.
6. Keep decorative detail away from small copy.

Risk: a light background can feel empty or washed out. Correct it with stronger scale, grounding shadows, restrained structural shapes, or one clear accent—not random texture.

## 3. Dark backgrounds

Use a dark background for premium, dramatic, entertainment, technology, nightlife, luxury, finance, or high-impact promotional communication.

Construction:

1. Avoid a dead flat fill; use a controlled gradient, glow, vignette, or subtle texture.
2. Place the brightest region behind or near the focal subject.
3. Use light text with enough contrast.
4. Separate dark hair, clothing, products, or devices with rim light or a brighter backing shape.
5. Keep shadows visible; do not crush all dark values into one mass.
6. Limit glowing effects so important information remains dominant.

Risk: excessive glow and saturation make every object compete. Use one principal light source and one supporting accent.

## 4. Gradient backgrounds

A gradient should direct attention or create depth, not simply decorate.

- Bright centre behind a product creates focus.
- Dark-to-light side transition supports split layouts.
- Top-to-bottom darkening can create a natural footer zone.
- Radial gradients support centred compositions.
- Diagonal gradients reinforce motion.

Keep transitions smooth unless a banded graphic style is intentional.

## 5. Photographic backgrounds

Use when location or context is part of the message: campus, office, city, shop, event venue, home, or travel.

To protect readability:

- Blur or soften distant detail.
- Apply haze or a restrained overlay.
- Reduce contrast behind text.
- Crop the photograph around the text zone.
- Keep strong lines from passing through letters or faces.
- Match the subject’s perspective and light direction to the scene.

Do not use a photograph solely because it is attractive. It should explain where the action happens or what the brand represents.

## 6. Textured backgrounds

Textures may include grain, paper, halftone, fabric, smoke, flour, dust, or subtle pattern.

Use one primary texture family. Apply it across both graphic and photographic elements when the goal is to unify mixed assets.

Texture should be felt before it is studied. Reduce it if it competes with body copy or product labels.

## 7. Abstract and geometric backgrounds

Use curves, circles, ribbons, lines, cards, grids, or interface-inspired shapes to organise space.

Each shape should have a role:

- Frame the hero.
- Create a text panel.
- Connect products.
- Direct the eye.
- Form a CTA.
- Separate the footer.

Do not add unrelated shapes merely to fill empty corners.

## 8. Background complexity rule

- More text → quieter background.
- More products → simpler structural shapes.
- One simple product → background may carry more atmosphere.
- Energetic campaign → controlled texture and motion are acceptable.
- Premium campaign → fewer elements and more tonal restraint.
- Small-screen output → remove detail that disappears or becomes noise.

## 9. Contrast system

Test four forms of contrast:

1. **Value contrast:** light against dark.
2. **Scale contrast:** dominant headline or hero against smaller support.
3. **Edge contrast:** crisp focal subject against softer background.
4. **detail contrast:** detailed product or face against a quieter zone.

A colour difference without enough value difference may still be unreadable.

## 10. Depth construction

Build depth through:

- Foreground, middle ground, and background.
- Scale reduction with distance.
- Controlled blur.
- Overlap.
- Cast shadows.
- Atmospheric haze.
- Perspective lines.
- Objects exiting the canvas.

Depth should support hierarchy. Do not blur important products, faces, logos, or text.

## 11. Lighting logic

All composited elements should appear to share an environment.

Check:

- Direction of light.
- Warmth or coolness.
- Highlight strength.
- Shadow direction.
- Edge light.
- Contact shadow where objects touch surfaces.
- Reflected environmental colour.

A person lit from the left should not be placed in a scene whose strongest light clearly comes from the right unless relit intentionally.

## 12. Integrating cut-outs

For every inserted subject or product:

1. Clean the cut-out edge without making it unnaturally sharp.
2. Match overall contrast and colour grading.
3. Add a contact or cast shadow.
4. Add environmental rim light where needed.
5. Match sharpness and grain to the scene.
6. Confirm the perspective and camera height are believable.
7. Preserve important product labels and facial detail.

Avoid the sticker effect: a clean cut-out with no shared light, shadow, texture, or atmospheric treatment.

## 13. Text protection methods

If text competes with the background:

- Move it into a quieter zone.
- Darken or lighten the local area.
- Add a subtle gradient behind it.
- Use a simple backing shape.
- Reduce background sharpness.
- Shorten the text block.
- Change the composition instead of adding multiple shadows and outlines.

## 14. Background selection map

- **Corporate/service:** light gradient, faint context, restrained structure.
- **Technology:** dark or bright digital gradient, radial glow, curved platforms.
- **Food:** contextual surface, ingredient cues, appetising directional light.
- **Education:** clean light field, campus/library context, clear text zones.
- **Entertainment:** dark atmosphere, controlled glow, dynamic texture.
- **Luxury:** tonal depth, minimal texture, precise lighting.
- **Celebration:** brighter atmosphere, controlled decorative motifs, clear greeting.
- **Finance:** clean or deep branded environment, product interface, trust-building clarity.

## Final test

Temporarily remove the subject and text. The background should still show where the message and hero belong. Then return the content: the background should become secondary.

## Core rule

> Build the environment around the communication, not the communication on top of an unrelated environment.
